# PART 1: ASEAN LENDING CRITERIA (2026)

## 1.1 Overview of MSME Lending Landscape in Malaysia

Malaysia's MSME lending landscape in 2026 is shaped by three tiers:
- **Tier 1 — Traditional Banks:** Maybank, CIMB, RHB, Public Bank, Hong Leong
- **Tier 2 — Development Finance Institutions (DFIs):** BPMB, SME Bank, Agrobank, Bank Rakyat
- **Tier 3 — Digital Banks & Fintech Lenders:** GXBank, AEON Bank, Boost Bank, Funding Societies, CapBay

Each tier has distinct lending criteria, eligibility rules, and documentation requirements.

# PART 4: COMPLIANCE UPDATES (2026)

## 4.1 E-Invoicing (MyInvois) — Malaysia Mandatory Rollout

### 4.1.1 What Is E-Invoicing?
E-Invoicing (or electronic invoicing) in Malaysia refers to the **MyInvois system** managed by the **Inland Revenue Board of Malaysia (LHDN / IRB)**. All invoices, credit notes, debit notes, and refund notes must be issued digitally through MyInvois and validated in real-time by LHDN.

This replaces traditional paper or PDF invoices sent directly to buyers. Every transaction is now reported to LHDN at the point of issuance.

---

### 4.1.2 E-Invoicing Rollout Timeline (Mandatory Phases)

| Phase | Who | Effective Date |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | Businesses with annual turnover > RM 100 million | 1 August 2024 |
| Phase 2 | Businesses with annual turnover > RM 25 million | 1 January 2025 |
| Phase 3 | **ALL remaining businesses (including MSMEs)** | **1 July 2025** |

> **Important:** As of July 2025, ALL businesses in Malaysia regardless of size are required to issue e-invoices via MyInvois for B2B transactions. B2C transactions have a simplified consolidated e-invoice option.

---

### 4.1.3 How MyInvois Works (Step-by-Step for MSMEs)

**Step 1 — Registration**
- Register at mytax.hasil.gov.my using your business Tax Identification Number (TIN)
- Link your SSM registration number
- Obtain your MyInvois digital certificate (free)

**Step 2 — Choose Your Integration Method**
- **Option A (Free):** Use the MyInvois Portal directly at einvoice.hasil.gov.my — manual entry, suitable for businesses issuing < 100 invoices/month
- **Option B (Recommended):** Integrate your accounting software (SQL, AutoCount, QuickBooks Malaysia, Xero) with MyInvois API
- **Option C (Advanced):** Direct API integration for businesses with custom ERP/billing systems

**Step 3 — Issuing an E-Invoice**
1. Create invoice in your system with all mandatory fields
2. Submit to MyInvois portal/API for validation
3. LHDN validates within 300 milliseconds (real-time)
4. Invoice gets a **Unique Identifier Number (UIN)** and QR code
5. Send validated e-invoice (with QR) to your buyer
6. Buyer has 72 hours to reject; after that it is deemed accepted

**Step 4 — For B2C (Retail / Consumer Transactions)**
- You may issue a **Consolidated E-Invoice** once per month covering all B2C sales
- Individual B2C receipts (via POS, Shopee, Grab) do not need separate e-invoices in real-time
- Consolidate by end of each calendar month and submit to MyInvois

---

### 4.1.4 Mandatory Fields in a MyInvois E-Invoice

- Supplier name, TIN, SSM number, address
- Buyer name, TIN, SSM number (for B2B), address
- Invoice date and time
- Invoice number (unique, sequential)
- Description of goods/services
- Unit price, quantity, subtotal
- SST (if applicable) — rate and amount
- Total amount payable
- Payment terms and method

---

### 4.1.5 Penalties for Non-Compliance

- **First Offence:** RM 200 – RM 20,000 fine per invoice not issued correctly
- **Repeat Offence:** Up to RM 50,000 per invoice + possible imprisonment
- **Practical Risk:** Tax deductions for buyers may be rejected if supplier has not issued a valid e-invoice — meaning your business clients will push you to comply or stop doing business with you

---

### 4.1.6 E-Invoicing for ASEAN Cross-Border Transactions

- Malaysia's MyInvois uses the **PEPPOL international standard**, meaning it is compatible with Singapore's InvoiceNow, Thailand's e-Tax Invoice & Receipt, and other ASEAN e-invoicing systems
- Cross-border B2B invoices to Singapore: Can be transmitted via PEPPOL network if both parties are registered
- Benefit: Eliminates customs paperwork duplication for ASEAN trades

## 4.2 ESG & Sustainability Reporting for MSMEs

### 4.2.1 Why MSMEs Need ESG in 2026

ESG (Environmental, Social, Governance) is no longer just for listed companies. In 2026, MSMEs in Malaysia are increasingly required to provide basic ESG statements or sustainability data because:

1. **Grant eligibility** — Multiple 2026 grants (MIDA, MDEC, SME Corp) now require a basic ESG self-declaration
2. **Supply chain requirements** — Large corporate and MNC buyers (Nestle, Unilever, Petronas, Sime Darby) require Tier-1 suppliers to submit annual sustainability reports
3. **Banking requirements** — Banks offering "Green Financing" products require basic ESG metrics to offer preferential rates
4. **Export market access** — EU CBAM (Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism) and Singapore GreenMark requirements affect export-oriented MSMEs

---

### 4.2.2 ESG Framework Simplified for MSMEs

MSMEs do NOT need a full corporate sustainability report. A **Basic MSME ESG Statement** covers three pillars:

#### Pillar E — Environmental
What to track and report:
- **Energy consumption:** Monthly electricity usage (kWh) — get from TNB bill
- **Water consumption:** Monthly water usage (m³) — get from utility bill
- **Waste generation:** Estimate monthly solid waste (kg) and recycling rate
- **Carbon footprint estimate:** Use MDEC's free SME Carbon Calculator at mdec.com.my
- **Green initiatives:** Any actions taken (LED lighting switch, reduced plastic packaging, recycling programme)

#### Pillar S — Social
What to track and report:
- **Employee count:** Full-time, part-time, contract
- **Employee demographics:** Gender ratio, age group, Bumiputera/non-Bumiputera split
- **Training hours:** Average training hours per employee per year (HRDC records)
- **Workplace safety incidents:** Number of accidents or near-misses per year
- **Community contributions:** Charitable donations, local community hire, apprenticeship programmes
- **Supplier diversity:** Local vs. imported suppliers ratio

#### Pillar G — Governance
What to track and report:
- **Business registration status:** SSM compliance, valid licences
- **Tax compliance:** LHDN filing up to date, e-invoicing compliance
- **Anti-corruption policy:** Written policy (even 1 page) — required for some grants
- **Data privacy practice:** PDPA compliance (Malaysia Personal Data Protection Act 2010)
- **Director/owner background:** No criminal record declaration (standard for most grants)

## 5.2 MSME Legal & Regulatory Compliance Checklist

### 5.2.1 Business Registration (SSM)
- **Sole Proprietorship / Partnership:** RM 30/year registration fee

### 5.2.2 Tax Obligations
- **Income Tax (LHDN):**
  - Sole Prop: File Form B, deadline 30 June each year
  - Sdn Bhd: File Form C, within 7 months of financial year-end
  - Estimated tax (CP204): Must submit estimate within 3 months of FY start
- **Sales & Service Tax (SST):**
  - Register for SST when annual taxable sales > RM 500,000
  - SST rate: 6% (services), 5% or 10% (goods depending on category)
  - File bimonthly SST-02 return
- **E-Invoicing (MyInvois):** Mandatory from July 2025 for all businesses (see Part 4.1)

### 5.2.3 Employment Law Essentials
- **EPF (KWSP):** Employer contributes 13% (employers with payroll < RM 20,000/month) or 12% of employee's salary
- **SOCSO (PERKESO):** Employer contributes 1.75% of employee's salary
- **EIS (Employment Insurance System):** Employer contributes 0.4% of employee's salary
- **Minimum Wage 2026:** RM 1,700/month (Peninsula Malaysia); RM 1,700/month (Sabah, Sarawak, Labuan) — unified in 2024
- **Annual Leave:** Minimum 8 days (< 2 years service), 12 days (2–5 years), 16 days (> 5 years)

# PART 6: FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT FOR MSMES

## 6.1 Cash Flow Management Basics

### 6.1.1 The 3-Account System (Best Practice for MSMEs)
Every MSME should maintain three separate bank accounts:

- **Account 1 — Operations Account:** All income received here; all bills paid from here
- **Account 2 — Tax Reserve Account:** Transfer 10%–15% of every income received; untouchable until tax payment due
- **Account 3 — Savings / Emergency Fund:** Build up to 3× monthly fixed costs; used only for genuine emergencies

### 6.1.2 Key Cash Flow Ratios to Monitor

- **Current Ratio:** Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities. Healthy: > 1.5
- **Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC):** Days Inventory + Days Receivable − Days Payable. Target: < 30 days for most MSMEs
- **Runway:** Cash in bank ÷ Monthly burn rate. Should always be > 3 months
- **Receivables Turnover:** Revenue ÷ Average Accounts Receivable. Higher = faster collection = better cash flow

### 6.1.3 Late Payment: Your Legal Rights (Malaysia)
- **Interest on Late B2B Payments:** Under Malaysia's Prompt Payment (Amendment) Act 2023, businesses can charge 8% p.a. interest on overdue invoices (automatic right, no need to state in contract)
- **Adjudication (AIAC):** For construction/service disputes > RM 5,000, can refer to Asian International Arbitration Centre (AIAC) adjudication — faster than court

## 6.2 Credit Score Building for MSMEs

### 6.2.1 CCRIS (Central Credit Reference Information System)
- **Managed by:** Bank Negara Malaysia
- **What it tracks:** All credit facilities (loans, overdrafts, credit cards, hire purchase) with licensed financial institutions
- **How to access:** Free check via BNM eLINK (bnmlink.bnm.gov.my) or any BNM branch
- **Clean CCRIS tip:** Ensure no 3-consecutive-month missed payments; even RM 1 overdue counts as a default month

### 6.2.2 CTOS (Private Credit Bureau)
- **What it tracks:** Court judgements, bankruptcy notices, CCRIS summary, trade references, directorship records
- **How to check:** ctos.com.my — free basic report, premium report from RM 25
- **CTOS Score Range:** 300–850 (similar to FICO); above 697 is considered "good" for loan applications

### 6.2.3 Credit Score Improvement Actions
1. Register all business loans under company name (not personal name) — separates business credit from personal
2. Maintain at least 1 active trade credit account (credit terms with a supplier) and pay on time
3. Keep utilisation of any credit line below 30%
4. File taxes on time — LHDN compliance reflects positively in alternative credit scoring
5. Issue e-invoices consistently — LHDN transaction data is increasingly used as alternative credit evidence (2026 update)

## FAQ: Grants & Funding

**Q: What grants are available for my F&B business in 2026?**  
A: Several grants are available for F&B businesses: (1) SME Corp Digitalisation Grant — up to RM 5,000 for digital tools; (2) JAKIM Halal certification fee subsidy up to RM 2,000; (3) FAMA Agri-Digitalisation Grant up to RM 8,000 if you have agricultural inputs; (4) HRDC training grants to upskill your staff. If you're Bumiputera-owned, also explore TEKUN micro loans (RM 50,000 at 4% p.a.) and MARA Digital Grant (RM 10,000). Apply at smecorp.gov.my first.

**Q: What grants are available for tech startups or digital businesses?**  
A: The best options are: (1) Cradle Fund CIP Catalyst — up to RM 150,000 non-dilutive grant for tech product validation; (2) MDEC Go eCommerce — vouchers + training for going digital; (3) SME Corp BAP — up to RM 20,000 for business acceleration. Also check MaGIC (Malaysia Global Innovation & Creativity Centre) for ecosystem programmes.

**Q: How do I apply for the SME Corp Digitalisation Grant?**  
A: Register at smecorp.gov.my, create a business profile, submit your grant application under "Digitalisation Grant," attach: (1) SSM certificate, (2) latest 3 months bank statement, (3) quotation from approved tech vendor. Processing takes 4–8 weeks. It is a reimbursement grant — you pay first, then claim back up to RM 5,000 (50% of total spend).

**Q: I have no credit history. Can I still get a loan?**  
A: Yes. Options with lower credit requirements: (1) Boost Bank — approves with only 3 months operating history and Boost transaction data; (2) GXBank — uses GrabMerchant sales data as alternative credit; (3) CGC Direct Lending — designed for new businesses with no formal credit; (4) TEKUN — no collateral needed for micro loans under RM 10,000. Start small, repay consistently, and your credit profile will build within 6–12 months.

**Q: What is the easiest and fastest loan to get in Malaysia for an MSME?**  
A: Boost Bank BizCredit is typically the fastest — approval within 24 hours, minimum 3 months history needed, amounts from RM 1,000. GXBank is second fastest if you have Grab merchant history. For larger amounts with competitive rates, BPMB Business Assist (from 4% p.a.) takes 2–4 weeks but offers the best rates for eligible MSMEs.

**Q: What documents do I need to apply for a bank loan?**  
A: Standard requirements: (1) MyKAD of business owner/directors; (2) SSM Business Registration Certificate; (3) 6 months bank statements (all accounts); (4) Latest 2 years audited or management accounts; (5) Business premises tenancy agreement; (6) Latest income tax return (Form B or Form C). Digital banks (GXBank, Boost, AEON) typically need only MyKAD + SSM cert + 3 months bank statements.

## FAQ: E-Invoicing (MyInvois)

**Q: Is e-invoicing compulsory for my small business?**  
A: Yes. From 1 July 2025, ALL businesses in Malaysia including micro-businesses and sole proprietors must issue e-invoices through LHDN's MyInvois system. The penalty for non-compliance ranges from RM 200 to RM 50,000 per non-compliant invoice.

**Q: How do I start with e-invoicing? I'm not tech-savvy.**  
A: The simplest way: (1) Go to einvoice.hasil.gov.my; (2) Log in with your MyTax credentials (same as LHDN); (3) Use the free MyInvois Portal to manually key in invoices. For volume above 50 invoices/month, subscribe to Bukku (RM 30/month) or SQL Accounting — both are certified and have simple step-by-step wizards. Watch LHDN's official tutorial at hasil.gov.my/einvoice.

**Q: Do I need to e-invoice my individual (B2C) customers?**  
A: For B2C retail transactions, you can issue a Consolidated E-Invoice once per month covering all your individual customer sales for that month. You do not need to issue individual e-invoices to each walk-in customer or online shopper. Consolidate your Shopee, Grab, walk-in sales at month-end and submit one consolidated record to MyInvois.

**Q: My customer is asking for an e-invoice. How do I send it to them?**  
A: (1) Issue the invoice via your accounting software or MyInvois portal; (2) The invoice is validated by LHDN within seconds and gets a QR code; (3) Download the validated PDF and send it to your customer via WhatsApp or email. The QR code on the invoice is how your customer verifies it is genuine.

**Q: Can I still send PDF invoices by email?**  
A: You must first validate through MyInvois, then you can send the validated PDF (with QR code) by email. A plain unvalidated PDF invoice no longer satisfies the legal requirement for B2B transactions.

## FAQ: ESG & Sustainability

**Q: What is ESG and why does my small business need it?**  
A: ESG stands for Environmental, Social, and Governance. In 2026 Malaysia, your small business needs a basic ESG statement because: (1) Many government grants now require a simple ESG declaration; (2) Large companies that buy from you (Tier-1 corporate buyers) increasingly require suppliers to report ESG data; (3) Green financing products (lower interest rate loans) require basic ESG metrics. You don't need a complex report — a 1-page self-declaration covering your energy use, employee data, and compliance status is sufficient for most MSME purposes.

**Q: How do I create an ESG report for my business?**  
A: Use the free template in Part 4.2.3 of this knowledge base. You need to collect: (1) Your TNB electricity bills for the year; (2) Water bill totals; (3) Employee headcount and demographics; (4) Training hours from HRDC records; (5) Confirm your SSM, LHDN, and PDPA compliance status. Compile these into a 1-page declaration signed by the business owner. This satisfies most grant and procurement ESG requirements.

**Q: What is the Green Financing I keep hearing about?**  
A: Bank Negara Malaysia's Low Carbon Transition Facility (LCTF) provides SME loans at below-market rates (from 3.5% p.a.) specifically for green investments — solar panels, energy-efficient equipment, electric vehicles for delivery, sustainable packaging machinery. Access through Maybank, CIMB, RHB, or AmBank. You need to submit an ESG self-declaration and basic energy audit to qualify.

## FAQ: ASEAN Export

**Q: How do I start selling to Singapore?**  
A: Steps: (1) Register with MATRADE at matrade.gov.my (free for Malaysian companies); (2) Get a Certificate of Origin (COO) from MATRADE or your chamber of commerce — this gives you 0% tariff under AFTA; (3) List on Shopee SG or Lazada SG as a cross-border seller; (4) For larger volumes, find a Singapore distributor through MATRADE's TradeMatch database; (5) Open a SGD-capable bank account (Wise or Maybank SG) to receive payments without FX losses.

**Q: Do I need Halal certification to export to ASEAN?**  
A: It depends on the destination. It is mandatory or strongly recommended for: Indonesia (Muslim majority, strict Halal requirements), Malaysia's Muslim consumer market, Middle East (beyond ASEAN), and Brunei. For Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam, Philippines — Halal cert is a strong competitive advantage but not legally mandatory for all products. For F&B and cosmetics, always get JAKIM Halal cert before targeting Muslim-majority markets.

**Q: What is MATRADE and how can it help me?**  
A: MATRADE (Malaysia External Trade Development Corporation) is the national export promotion agency. Free services for Malaysian exporters include: (1) TradeMatch — connects you with overseas buyers; (2) Market Development Grant (MDG) — up to RM 300,000 reimbursement for export marketing expenses; (3) Trade missions and international trade fair participation support; (4) Export readiness assessment and coaching. Register free at matrade.gov.my.

## FAQ: General Business Operations

**Q: What is the minimum wage in Malaysia 2026?**  
A: RM 1,700 per month (standardised across Peninsula Malaysia, Sabah, Sarawak, and Labuan as of 2024). This is the minimum for all employees including part-time workers (calculated pro-rata). Interns on a formal internship programme (with university MOU) are exempt from minimum wage if stipend is clearly stated as an internship allowance.

**Q: Do I need to pay EPF for part-time workers?**  
A: Yes, EPF contribution is mandatory for all employees (full-time and part-time) who are Malaysian citizens or Permanent Residents, regardless of hours worked. Employer contributes 13% (for employees earning ≤ RM 5,000/month) or 12% (above RM 5,000). Employee contributes 11%. For foreign workers, EPF is optional but SOCSO is mandatory.

**Q: How do I calculate if my business is profitable enough to hire my first employee?**  
A: Rule of thumb: Monthly revenue should be at least 4× the total employment cost (salary + EPF 13% + SOCSO 1.75% + EIS 0.4%). Example: hiring someone at RM 1,800/month has a total cost of ~RM 2,120/month; your monthly revenue should be at least RM 8,480 and growing before this hire is sustainable. Always model the revenue uplift that the new hire is expected to generate.

**Q: My business is losing money every month. What should I do first?**  
A: Prioritise in this order: (1) Identify your top 3 revenue sources and double down on them — stop spreading thin; (2) Run a cost audit — categorise all expenses as "must have," "nice to have," or "cut immediately"; (3) Review your pricing — most early-stage MSMEs undercharge; model your true cost including your own time at market rate; (4) Improve collections — if you have outstanding invoices, collect those first before selling more; (5) Talk to an SME Corp Business Advisor (free counselling at smecorp.gov.my). Running at a loss for > 6 months without a clear path to break-even requires decisive action.
